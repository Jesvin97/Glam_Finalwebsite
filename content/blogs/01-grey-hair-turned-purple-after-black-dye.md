# Why Did My Grey Hair Turn Purple After Dyeing It Black?

**Short answer:** Black hair dye is built on blue and violet pigments. On grey or white hair, which is porous and has no natural colour underneath, those cool pigments can show up as a purple or bluish tint instead of a deep black. It is a tone problem, not a damage problem, and a stylist can usually correct it.

## Why grey hair behaves differently

Dark hair has natural pigment that mixes with the dye and balances it. Grey hair has none. It also tends to be coarser and more porous, so it grabs cool pigments quickly. Add sunlight, hard water and frequent washing, and the blue-violet base becomes more visible within days.

## Common reasons it turns purple

- The shade was a cool "blue-black" rather than a natural black or dark brown.
- The dye was left on too long or applied over already coloured hair.
- Hair was lightly lightened or damaged before, so it holds more cool tone.
- Harsh shampoos stripped the warm part of the shade and left the cool part.

## How to fix it

1. Do not keep re-dyeing at home. Stacking more black on top can make the tint stronger.
2. Wait a few washes with a gentle, colour-safe shampoo. Mild tints often settle.
3. If the purple stays, see a colourist. They can balance it with a warmer natural brown or black so grey strands look even with the rest.

## How to avoid it next time

Ask for a natural black or dark brown with warm balance. Tell your stylist about any earlier colour or henna, and request a strand test before the full application.

## FAQ

**Will the purple tint wash out?** Often it fades a little, but a strong tint usually needs a corrective tone.

**Is purple a sign of damage?** No. It is a colour tone issue, though already damaged hair can show it more.

**Can I fix it at home?** It is safer to see a colourist than to add more dye on top.
